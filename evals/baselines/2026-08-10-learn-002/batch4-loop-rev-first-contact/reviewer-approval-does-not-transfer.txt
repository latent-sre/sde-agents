**Verdict: does not transfer.** A formal approval binds to an exact identity triple — `candidate_sha` / `base_sha` / `tree_oid` — and applies only to that identity, never to another SHA "however small the delta." A rebase, even a one-line reparent with byte-identical diff content, produces:

- a new `candidate_sha` (different commit object — different parent, different committer timestamp at minimum)
- a new `base_sha` (the "newer base" is by definition not `bbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbb`)
- almost certainly a new `tree_oid` if the base moved at all in the reviewed files' ancestry — but even if the tree_oid happened to be byte-identical, the triple as a whole has changed, and the rule is that the approval binds to the triple, not to any one field in it.

"The diff is identical" is a claim about content equivalence, not identity equivalence. The review discipline explicitly rejects that substitution: approval reads as coverage, and carrying it forward onto unseen-by-that-exact-review bytes is "worse than no approval because it reads as coverage" when it isn't. The newer base itself is unreviewed material — nothing in the original review examined what changed between `bbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbbb` and the new base, or whether that delta interacts with the rebased diff (a merge-base shift can silently change semantics even when the patch text is unchanged — e.g., a function it calls was modified upstream of the rebase point).
